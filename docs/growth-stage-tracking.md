# Growth-stage tracking V1

V1 scope stays small: harvest permanently ends living growth, one grow per plant record, editable dates, a visible estimate label, and review prompts calculated from stage milestones or seasonal conditions. There is no separate stale-stage timer, confidence-scoring feature or multi-cycle model. Confirmed dates are checked at the write boundary: emergence follows germination, flowering follows emergence, harvest follows flowering, and post-harvest stages follow harvest and the preceding confirmed stage. Estimated dates do not block correcting actual dates. A mistaken stage can be corrected while retaining its journal history.

The supplied specification ended at “Every” in the stage-history section. This implementation covers its supplied requirements without inventing the missing remainder.

`growthStage` remains the only authoritative stage. Existing `germination`, `unknown` and `clone` values remain compatible; `harvested`, `drying`, `curing` and `completed` extend the vocabulary. Deploy the updated Firestore rules before using these new stages in production. No production deployment or data migration is performed by this change.

Stage dates use the project's calendar-date convention (`stageStartedOn`, `emergedOn`, etc.) and `locationTimeZone`. Unknown age now receives an explicitly labelled planning estimate from the selected stage. New plants require a concrete stage and store a numeric age, an estimated total-age start date, and a separate estimated current-stage start. Legacy plants without usable age/stage timing get a derived estimate anchored to their saved recording/creation date, without a bulk database migration. Recording an actual stage date replaces the stage timing estimate. Changing a stage through either the card or the existing editor atomically writes its journal history. Harvest remains the existing dated harvest transaction and also records the stage transition.

The journal's Today view and Plant info contain the card. It shows elapsed days (labelled approximate when inferred), the next estimated check, observations and explicit confirmation controls. Harvest hands off to Manage plant → Record harvest. After harvest, users can finish tracking or opt into drying and maturation; the original growing algorithm stops. All date milestones remain suggestions and never change a plant automatically.

The pure `lib/plants/growthTracking.ts` evaluator covers germination (5/10 days), seedling (14/21), flowering progress (42), harvest review (56 by default, configurable), drying (7/14) and maturation (14/28). Rejection delays checks by at least seven days (21 for flowering checks), snooze delays seven days, and dismissal suppresses the stable stage-instance suggestion until reset. Pre-flowering is an observation within vegetative. Reviews are stored on the plant and in its journal.

Flowering response defaults to unknown. Seasonal suggestions require an explicitly selected photoperiod profile, outdoor coordinates, the user-supplied minimum 60 days in vegetative, and at least 35 days established (the original spec’s 4–5-week check). Both observed and clearly labelled estimated timing can support a soft check; neither confirms a transition. Daylight must be shortening across the preceding and following seven days, with the solar declination trend agreeing in that hemisphere. The earlier hard-coded January–April / July–October month windows and 10-degree latitude cutoff have been removed. A month filter only applies when explicitly configured by the user for a variety. Equatorial/polar flat daylight does not yield a confident seasonal signal; direct observed signs remain available. The calculation is a seasonal heuristic, not a biologically validated forecast. Missing profile/location gets a non-blocking setup card.

Setup and recording/correcting a current stage count as reviews (`stageReview.action: stage_recorded`). The next check uses a milestone strictly ahead of the reviewed stage age: an estimated day-18 seedling waits for day 21, and a day-70 flowering plant waits for day 77. Beyond the listed milestones, the original stage-specific repeat interval applies. An optional actual stage-start date during creation overrides the inferred stage clock and updates only unknown/contradictory estimated ages. Valid user-supplied total ages are preserved.

Flowering progress and harvest-readiness cards have separate stable identities. Dismissing progress cannot suppress the later readiness milestone; dismissing readiness continues suppressing it. A response to an earlier phase gives at least seven days of breathing room before a new phase. Progress checks stay in-app; the known future harvest-readiness push uses its readiness identity even when planned during the progress phase. When a recent progress response postpones the future readiness check, the original device cancels its own still-pending reminder and atomically moves that same reservation later. An elapsed reservation is never rearmed, and another device cannot use the normal planning path to duplicate it. Interruption during cancellation/rescheduling remains fail-closed: an optional push can be omitted, while the in-app check remains correctly postponed.

Users may record a dated early-flowering observation at any time while vegetative, without waiting for seasonal eligibility or a known flowering profile. This records evidence and a seven-day follow-up, leaves the stage vegetative, and takes priority over seasonal timing. Dates, ownership, activity and revision are checked atomically. There is no confidence score, image classifier, automatic stage advancement, or extra growing cycle.

## Device reminders

Stage notifications require both the existing account/device reminders preference and a plant's explicit stage-notification opt-in. The existing ReminderCoordinator and Expo notification architecture schedule date triggers while the app is open; no new background worker, cloud messaging service or automation is installed. A stable per-stage key and Firestore transaction claim prevent repeated evaluations, restarts and other devices from claiming duplicate pushes. One original push and at most one explicit snooze push are permitted, at least seven days apart. Responses invalidate the original push. Default quiet hours are 20:00–08:00 in the plant zone (device zone until resolved). Changed quiet hours or location invalidate pending reminders.

Stage pushes reserve at most three pending slots and respect a 63-notification ceiling. Active high-priority care hazards take precedence. Existing care/weather notifications keep their own identifiers and behavior. Stage notifications open the existing plant details route; all four response controls live on the suggestion card. Multiple plants remain separate notifications for reliable per-plant cancellation; grouping is a future enhancement.

Claims are deliberately conservative: an interruption or scheduling failure after a committed claim can omit an optional push rather than duplicate one. The in-app suggestion remains available. A device can cancel its own scheduled checks after receiving live plant changes; cancellation on another offline device takes effect when that device next syncs. New seasonal suggestions require the app to evaluate current conditions; they are in-app only. These are device reminders, not a newly deployed server push service.

## Hops guidance and provisional timing

Growing-duration ranges come from the user’s updated table: germination 3–10 days, seedling 14–21, vegetative 60–150+, pre-flowering transition 7–14, and flowering 56–84+. Drying 7–14 and curing 14–28+ retain the original supplied spec. These are the product’s configured estimates, not substituted research timings. Optional curing is retained as requested, without claiming it is a standard hops processing requirement. The guide uses physical maturity cues described by [Oregon State University Extension](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9115-growing-hops-home-garden): inspect several cones for papery feel, golden lupulin and aroma. Compare [University of Florida harvest guidance](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP578). Solar horizon geometry reference: [NOAA sunrise/sunset equations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF).

Tests cover calendar boundaries, all milestones, both hemispheres, missing/unknown characteristics, delayed reviews, dismissal, snooze limits, quiet hours, transaction ownership/conflicts/retries, native scheduling/cancellation, and explicit UI confirmation. The backup schema accepts the new stages and retains tracking and history fields.

## Stage-based age baseline

Choosing Unknown age means “estimate it for me.” `lib/plants/stageAgeDefaults.ts` stores the user-supplied duration ranges. The single-number estimate is the rounded midpoint of each finite nominal range: germination 7, seedling 18, vegetative 105, flowering 70, drying 11 and curing 21 days. Open-ended `+` windows stay open-ended in the UI and never impose a completion deadline. Cumulative total ages are the sum of preceding stage midpoints plus the current stage midpoint: 7, 25, 130 and 200 days for the four seed-grown stages. Total plant age and days in stage are labelled separately. These are editable estimates, not observed dates.

Pre-flowering remains an observation within vegetative. Its 7–14-day transition window is not added to total age a second time. An explicit early-signs observation produces a soft check after seven days, with no automatic stage change or extra push. Other stage reminder milestones retain the original spec separately from the duration estimates.

The user supplied a 7–21-day rooting range for the existing clone stage, giving a 14-day midpoint since cutting. The separate proposed clone vegetative range (14–120+) and broader clone lifecycle are deferred; the current focus is seed-grown outdoor photoperiod plants. No outside research was used to select these day ranges.

Harvest is terminal for living growth. Restore-to-active is blocked by the app APIs and Firestore rules. Harvest-date corrections remain available without restoring growth, and optional drying/curing remain processing stages with harvested status.

`ageEstimateSource: stage` identifies an automatic age estimate; `stageStartedOnEstimated` separately identifies inferred stage timing. Profiles/review surfaces show these as estimates. Unknown-age legacy records receive a numeric planning age from the shared age helper; a legacy unknown stage uses zero as a tracking baseline until a concrete stage is recorded. No stage is automatically advanced, and inferred dates are never written as observed emergence or flowering dates.

## Verification scenarios for next-stage suggestions

Scenario tests cover no immediate setup prompt, observed stage-date priority, both hemispheres, no implicit seasonal month/latitude cutoffs, explicit variety overrides, the 60-day vegetative minimum, physical evidence taking priority, missing information, progress versus readiness dismissals, cross-phase cooldowns, stable future reminder identifiers, cancellation after review, and atomic real-Firestore observation/confirmation writes. No external horticultural research was used for these rule changes. Biological state still requires user observation and confirmation.

Actual stage dates also set a minimum for an explicitly estimated total age, so a guessed three-day-old plant cannot remain three days old when its observed current stage began ten days ago. Valid known dates are validated for consistency rather than overwritten. Correcting an inferred current-stage date recalculates only the inferred total age; changing to the next stage does not fabricate a new observed birth date. Legacy inferred stage dates use the creation date before an age-recording date, so editing total age does not shift the stage clock. Estimated flowering prompts explicitly identify their estimated basis. Users may record a harvest manually before a suggested readiness check, with terminal-lifecycle and date validation still enforced.
